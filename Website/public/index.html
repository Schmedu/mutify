The rule

One decision, made the same way every time.

Mutify mutes when the sound would come out of speakers the room can hear, on a network you haven't vouched for. That's the whole of it. Change either half below and watch the verdict move.

Headphones

Never touched.

If the sound is going into your ears, nobody else can hear it, so there is nothing to mute. AirPods, a USB headset, anything in the jack — Mutify leaves them alone in the library, in the café, anywhere.

Everything else is classified for you: built-in speakers and a monitor over HDMI count as room-audible. Odd virtual and USB devices get a row on the Devices tab, and you decide.

Two lists

Deny wins.

Put the networks where sound is fine on the allow list. Put the ones where it never is — the library, the office, an airport — on the mute list. When a network is somehow on both, the mute list wins.

  • On the allow listSound allowed
  • On the mute listMuted
  • On neither, or no networkYour call — muted by default
  • Nothing identifies itStands down, with a warning

That last row matters: Mutify never mutes on a guess.

Getting out of it

Five ways to say “not now”.

Allow sound for fifteen minutes, half an hour, an hour, until tomorrow morning, or until you leave this network — plus an indefinite pause for the days you just want it to stop.

A volume Mutify lowered is put back when sound is allowed again, on the device it lowered, and only if you haven't changed it yourself in the meantime.

Privacy

Nothing about your location or your networks ever leaves the machine.

There is no account, no server and no analytics — there's nowhere for any of it to go. macOS won't hand out Wi-Fi names without Location access, so Mutify identifies a network by its router's hardware address instead, which needs no permission at all and tells two cafés called FRITZ!Box apart. Grant Location access and you get the real names too.
